Website Evaluations

Playbook (Good Design)

  • Contrast: Clear distinction between the warm sunset hues and white header/body on the landing page. Scrolling down the webpage reveals a clean white background with crisp black text.
  • Repetition: Consistent visual elements throughout the page - sans font as a caption, a header that offers a summary, small body text, and a visually pleasing image. Playbook's mission is to organize images and visual files for access by AI agents, and caption text frequently refers to that idea, for example: "BUILT FOR VISUAL ACTIONS, READY FOR AGENTS." or "SEARCH & AI ACTIONS."
  • Alignment: Elements are padded to keep them in the center of the customer's vision at all times. Headers and body are laid out relatively close to each other from the left (i.e., header text doesn't stray too far from body text, and vice versa).
  • Proximity: Small, consistent padding keeps elements closer together without being cluttered. Each playbook feature has multimedia attached closely to it.

Bruto (Bad Design)

  • Contrast: Admittedly good - between the brick-like #cea47a background and #ffffff text, it's easy to read the content on the page, at least color-wise.
  • Repetition: Poor. While the headings(?) on the right are themed around bricks, there's no repetition related to the purpose of the webpage - a restaurant. Given websites should focus on communicating relevant information, this is a failure.
  • Alignment: Also very poor. An explanatory paragrpah is found in the bottom right, and a numbered list is in the top right with a larger font. There's also a footer that obscures part of the bottom paragraph. In other words, no alignment or structure to the elements in the webpage.
  • Proximity: The worst part of this webpage. Too many visually contrasting elements, including different fonts, and dotted liens that make the explanatory paragraph (about the meaning of Brutø) difficult to read. The webpage presents itelf as an explosion of modern font and smooth graphics, with zero relevance to the point of the website - to advertise a restaurant.
